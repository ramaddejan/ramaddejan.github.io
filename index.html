About Our Project

We are a group of ICT first-year students from Tshwane University of Technology, working on a project to support the Sustainable Development Goals of No Poverty and Zero Hunger. Our focus is to research food garden projects in South Africa and share ways to improve food security in communities.

Our Mission

Local Humanitarians aims to empower South African communities by promoting sustainable food gardens, ensuring access to healthy food, and reducing poverty through teamwork and education.

Research Findings: Food Garden Projects in South Africa

1. Abalimi Bezekhaya (Cape Town)

Abalimi Bezekhaya supports urban farmers in Cape Town’s townships by providing training and resources. They help communities grow vegetables like spinach and tomatoes, improving food access for over 3,000 families annually.

2. Siyakhana Initiative (Johannesburg)

The Siyakhana Initiative focuses on organic farming in Johannesburg. They work with schools and clinics to grow food, teaching people how to farm sustainably. They’ve helped reduce hunger for 500+ households.

3. Ikhaya Garden (Khayelitsha)

Ikhaya Garden in Khayelitsha grows fresh vegetables for the community. They use recycled materials for planting and have fed over 200 families since starting in 2018.

4. Mamelodi Community Garden (Pretoria)

This garden in Mamelodi, Pretoria, grows crops like maize and beans. It’s run by local women who share the harvest with schools and orphanages, supporting 300+ children monthly.

5. Green Shack Project (Eastern Cape)

The Green Shack Project in the Eastern Cape builds small gardens for rural families. They use permaculture methods and have helped 150 families grow their own food since 2020.

Recommendations for Improving Food Security

  • Increase government funding for community gardens to provide more tools and seeds for rural areas.
  • Offer free training programs on organic farming to teach communities sustainable gardening methods.
  • Partner with schools to create garden projects, teaching children about farming and healthy eating.
  • Encourage local businesses to donate resources like water tanks and compost to support garden projects.
  • Build more community centers where people can share knowledge and trade their harvests.
